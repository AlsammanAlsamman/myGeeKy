People

People worth following, not just famous ones

GitHub discovery mostly shows accounts that will never notice you. myGeeKy finds people through the repos in your field that they own and contribute to.

  • Skips celebrities, mass-followers, organizations and dormant accounts
  • A second list surfaces domain experts a follow-back score would bury
  • Click someone to open their profile. You follow by hand, always.
Projects & pulse

  • A daily momentum board: stars gained, commits, PyPI downloads, and who moved up
Signals NEW

Say hi without words

👋 📚 🤝 👀 🔥

Wave, say "I learn from your work", offer to collaborate, or give kudos to a repo. Signals travel through a small public repo on your own GitHub, so there's no server, no text and nothing to moderate. When it's mutual, you get a 🤝 handshake.

  • Emoji only, from a fixed set, so no spam and no harassment
  • Fully public and transparent, and you can block anyone
  • Opt-in, with its own token that can write to one repo only
The panel

Lives quietly on the edge of your screen

Always on top, three glass themes. Folded, it's a small icon that now and then lets out a few floating hearts. 💕

Privacy & trust

Suggest-only, by design

myGeeKy has no follow, star or fork code at all. You look, you click, you decide.

🔒 Read-only token

Your GitHub token only needs read access and lives in your OS keyring (Windows Credential Locker, macOS Keychain, Linux Secret Service), never in a file.

🏠 Your data stays yours

No server, no account, no telemetry. Your CV and history stay on your machine, or in a private GitHub repo you own if you turn on sync.

📖 Open source

MIT-licensed and on GitHub, with tests. Read exactly what it does, and change it if you like.

Get started

Up and running in two minutes

Install

On Windows, run the installer and click Next → Next → Finish. Anywhere else:

pip install mygeeky

Tell it who you are

Your GitHub username, plus your CV, ORCID or Scholar profile if you like.

mygeeky init

Open the panel

Suggestions, repos, your field's pulse and signals, all live. The first run sets up the GUI by itself.

mygeeky gui
